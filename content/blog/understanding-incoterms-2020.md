Incoterms (International Commercial Terms) define exactly who pays for what, and — just as importantly — at what point risk transfers from seller to buyer during a shipment. Getting the Incoterm wrong in a contract doesn't just cause confusion; it can mean you're liable for cargo you thought was the other party's responsibility.

The current version is **Incoterms 2020**, published by the International Chamber of Commerce. There are 11 terms total, but in China-origin trade, a handful come up constantly.

## EXW — Ex Works

**The seller's only obligation is to make the goods available at their premises** (factory, warehouse). The buyer arranges and pays for everything from that point forward: loading, export customs clearance, inland transport, ocean freight, import customs, and final delivery.

- **Lowest price on paper** — but the buyer takes on the most work and risk.
- Often mistaken for "cheapest overall," but once you add freight forwarder fees for export clearance (which the seller is usually better positioned to handle), EXW can end up costing more in practice than FOB.

## FOB — Free On Board

**The seller delivers the goods on board the vessel at the named port of origin**, clearing export customs along the way. Risk transfers to the buyer once the goods are loaded onto the ship.

- The most commonly used term for ocean freight from China, because the seller (who is local to the origin port) is best positioned to handle export documentation and inland transport to the port.
- The buyer is responsible for ocean freight, insurance, import duties, and destination handling.

## CIF — Cost, Insurance, and Freight

**The seller pays for the goods, ocean freight, and minimum insurance coverage to the named destination port** — but risk still transfers to the buyer once goods are loaded onto the vessel at origin, same as FOB. The seller pays for the freight; the buyer bears the risk during transit.

- This distinction trips people up constantly: **CIF does not mean the seller is responsible if something happens to the cargo at sea.** It only means the seller pre-paid the freight and a baseline insurance policy — if the cargo is damaged in transit, the buyer (as the insured party, depending on policy assignment) files the claim.
- Buyers should confirm the insurance coverage level — Incoterms 2020 only requires *minimum* coverage (Institute Cargo Clauses C) under CIF, which is fairly limited. If you want broader coverage, negotiate for CIP (Carriage and Insurance Paid To) instead, which requires the higher Institute Cargo Clauses A coverage.

## Quick comparison

| Term | Seller pays for | Risk transfers at |
|---|---|---|
| EXW | Nothing beyond making goods available | Seller's premises |
| FOB | Export clearance, inland transport, loading | Once loaded on vessel |
| CIF | Above + ocean freight + minimum insurance | Once loaded on vessel |
| DAP (Delivered at Place) | Everything to named destination (excl. import duty) | On arrival at destination |
| DDP (Delivered Duty Paid) | Everything including import duty | On arrival at destination |

## How this affects your landed cost calculation

Whichever Incoterm you're quoted under determines exactly which cost line items you need to add yourself to get to a true landed cost:

- Quoted **EXW**? You need to add inland transport to port, export clearance, ocean freight, insurance, import duty, VAT, and destination delivery.
- Quoted **FOB**? You need to add ocean freight, insurance, import duty, VAT, and destination delivery.
- Quoted **CIF**? You need to add import duty, VAT, and destination delivery — freight and basic insurance are already included.
